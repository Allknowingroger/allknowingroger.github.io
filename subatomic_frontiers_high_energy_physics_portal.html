STAGE 1
2030s – 2040s Target

FCC-ee: Electron–Positron Factory

A precision lepton collider serving as a Higgs, electroweak, and top-quark factory. Operating at center-of-mass collision energies across $Z$, $W$, $Higgs$ ($240\text{ GeV}$), and $t\bar{t}$ threshold ($365\text{ GeV}$).

  • Ultra-high luminosity per collision point
  • Civil & technical infrastructure shared for Stage 2
  • Total Construction Budget: ~15 Billion Swiss Francs
STAGE 2
Mid-Century Target

FCC-hh: Proton–Proton Hadron Collider

A massive hadron collider operating in the same 91 km tunnel, utilizing advanced superconducting magnets (16–20 Tesla) to reach an unprecedented collision energy of roughly $100\text{ TeV}$.

  • Direct discovery reach for heavy dark particles up to tens of TeV
  • High luminosity proton-proton and heavy ion operational modes
  • Synergy with heavy-ion physics (Lead & Oxygen/Neon ion runs)

International Muon Collider Collaboration (IMCC)

Towards a High-Energy Muon Collider

Muons ($\mu^\pm$) are 207 times heavier than electrons, suppressing synchrotron radiation losses while retaining the advantages of fundamental point-like lepton collisions. This allows a $10\text{ TeV}$ Muon Collider to offer physics reach comparable to a $100\text{ TeV}$ proton collider at a fraction of the physical footprint.

Stage 01: High Intensity Proton Driver

The muon collider complex begins with a powerful proton driver producing short, high-intensity proton pulses (~2-4 MW). These protons are accelerated and directed onto a high-Z liquid or solid target (such as liquid gallium or graphite) to produce a copious flux of charged pions ($\pi^\pm$).

SYNCHROTRON RADIATIVE ADVANTAGE
$\sim 10^9 \times$ Suppression

Because mass $m_\mu \approx 207\,m_e$, synchrotron energy loss per turn $(\propto E^4/m^4)$ is reduced by over a factor of $10^9$ compared to electron-positron storage rings.

EFFECTIVE COLLISION ENERGY
$10\text{ TeV } \mu\mu \equiv 100\text{ TeV } pp$

In proton colliders, total energy is shared among constituent valence quarks and gluons. In muon colliders, the full center-of-mass energy is available in the hard scattering interaction.

PHYSICAL FOOTPRINT
< 10 km Ring

A $10\text{ TeV}$ Muon Collider ring could fit directly on existing CERN or Fermilab sites, dramatically lowering civil engineering costs.

Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) Simulator

Anatomy of the Proton

Protons are composite subatomic particles (baryons) containing three valence quarks: two Up quarks (electric charge $+2/3 e$) and one Down quark (electric charge $-1/3 e$), bound tightly by gluons carrying color charge.

Total Charge: +1.0 e ($+2/3 + 2/3 - 1/3$)
Proton Charge Radius (Recent confirmation): $\approx 0.841\text{ fm}$ (Smaller Radius Confirmed)
Mass Contribution: ~99% from Gluon Field Energy ($\Delta E = mc^2$)

ALICE EXPERIMENT AT LHC

Lead-to-Gold Nuclear Transmutation

Observation of ultra-peripheral collisions stripping protons/neutrons from high-energy lead nuclei ($^{208}\text{Pb}$) to create short-lived gold isotopes in beam pipes.

QUANTUM INFORMATION

First Antimatter Qubit Demonstration

CERN antiproton decelerator experiments successfully trapped antiprotons into quantum coherent superposition states for ultra-precise CPT symmetry testing.

HEAVY ION RUNS 2025/2026

Oxygen & Neon Ion Collisions

First-ever light-ion collision runs probing the geometry, cluster formation, and early-universe Quark-Gluon Plasma (QGP) onset dynamics.
