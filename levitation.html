EMF 2026 Summary

Levitation: From Hovercrafts to Holograms

Three technologies, one principle—eliminating physical contact to transform transportation, medicine, and human-computer interaction.

01

Magnetic Levitation

Magnetic levitation uses powerful superconducting magnets or electromagnets to lift and propel objects—typically vehicles—without physical contact, eliminating rolling friction entirely.

How it works

Systems rely on electromagnetic suspension (EMS), where electromagnets are attracted to a ferromagnetic guideway, or electrodynamic suspension (EDS), where superconducting magnets induce repulsive eddy currents in track coils. Because the train never touches the rail, wear and vibration are drastically reduced, enabling test speeds exceeding 600 km/h (373 mph).

Key characteristics

  • Uses strong magnetic fields for frictionless transport
  • Requires specialized guideway infrastructure with embedded coils
  • Ideal for intercity networks and high-speed corridors
  • Also applied in magnetic bearings for industrial turbines

02

Acoustic Levitation

Acoustic levitation uses high-intensity sound waves to trap small objects or liquid droplets in mid-air. Unlike magnetic methods, it does not require the object to be magnetic or conductive—only that it be significantly less dense than the surrounding medium.

How it works

An array of ultrasonic transducers generates a standing sound wave, creating fixed regions of high and low acoustic pressure called nodes and antinodes. Particles are pushed toward the pressure nodes—the stable points where net acoustic force balances gravity. By dynamically adjusting phase and amplitude, researchers can move these nodes in real time, translating the object through 3D space.

Key characteristics

  • Uses sound waves to create acoustic nodes
  • Traps small particles and liquid droplets without contact
  • Enables containerless processing for pure materials
  • Drives emerging volumetric 3D display technologies

03

Optical & Dynamic Levitation

At the microscopic scale, light itself can hold matter in place. At the macroscopic scale, a cushion of air can do the same. These two approaches share a simple idea: use a sustained pressure differential to counteract gravity.

Optical Levitation

Focused laser beams create intense optical gradients that draw dielectric particles toward the focal point. This is the principle behind optical tweezers, capable of manipulating individual cells with sub-nanometer precision.

Dynamic Levitation

Fans force high-pressure air beneath a skirted hull, creating an air cushion that lifts the vehicle above land or water. This enables amphibious transport and industrial air-bearing systems for fragile payloads.

Real-World Applications

Pharmaceuticals

Improved Drugs

Containerless levitation lets pharmaceutical researchers grow crystals without vessel walls introducing nucleation sites or impurities. The result: more stable, soluble, and bioavailable drug formulations—especially for amorphous compounds that are hard to manufacture cleanly.

Displays

Volumetric Displays

Rapid acoustic particle holograms move a tiny bead so fast it traces out a true 3D image in mid-air. Viewable from any angle without glasses, these displays also produce tactile feedback through acoustic pressure on skin—merging sight and touch.

Infrastructure

Transportation Networks

Maglev eliminates wheel-rail contact for frictionless intercity travel at speeds above 600 km/h. Meanwhile, air-cushion systems enable amphibious logistics and factory AGVs that move heavy, fragile loads without mechanical shock.
