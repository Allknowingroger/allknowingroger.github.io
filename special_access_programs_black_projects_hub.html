Definitive Definition

Black Projects vs. Special Access Programs (SAPs)

A Black Project is defined as a project whose existence is officially denied by all involved parties (defense contractors and government branches). It is funded through concealed lines in the defense budget, publicly reported only as lump sums with no description.

In contrast, a Special Access Program (SAP) is a formal security classification protocol within the Department of Defense (DoD) that enforces enhanced safeguarding and access requirements beyond standard Top Secret, Secret, or Confidential clearance levels.

standard Levels: TS / Secret / Confidential Strict Need-to-Know Enforcement Annual Congressional Reporting
1980s Era Terminology
"Black Programs"

Originally restricted primarily to DoD acquisition programs (e.g., Lockheed Skunk Works F-117A Stealth Fighter).

1990s Transition
SAP Integration

Replaced "Black Program". Modified to cover Intelligence Programs, Operations, and Operations Support.

Catalyst Event
Operation Yellow Fruit

1980s covert special ops scandal involving illegal fund usage & court-martials, forcing major Congressional oversight reforms.

The Fundamental Truth of Black Projects

"Because the defining feature is secrecy, every project in this list has one thing in common: It is here only because it was eventually declassified. The ones that are still running have no names yet."
  • Unacknowledged Status: Public officials will not confirm nor deny the program's existence.
  • Budget Concealment: Funded via lump-sum hidden line items in the defense budget ("Black Budget").
  • Restricted Briefings: Briefings to Congress are restricted to designated leadership without staff present.

Special Access Program (SAP) Categories

Classification taxonomy defined by DoD & CDSE guidelines. Click cards to view reporting requirements.

Category I

Acknowledged SAP

Program whose existence and general purpose may be openly recognized and acknowledged to the public or family.

Existence: Unclassified / Publicly Known
Technical Details: Strictly Classified
Program Funding: Generally Unclassified
Permitted Disclosure: Personnel may state they work for the SAP, but cannot disclose technical specifics.
Category II

Unacknowledged SAP

Program whose existence, purpose, technologies, and contractor involvement are protected and kept classified.

Existence: Classified
Purpose & Tech: Classified
Funding: Classified or unacknowledged budget line
Congressional Oversight: Requires standard annual reporting to congressional defense committees.
Category III (Waived)

Unacknowledged - Waived

An unacknowledged SAP for which the Secretary of Defense has formally waived standard Congressional reporting requirements due to extreme sensitivity.

Existence: Highly Classified
Standard Reporting: Waived by SECDEF
Briefing Restrictions: Limited to Select Committee Leaders ("Gang of Eight")
Special Handling: Dissemination control marker WAIVED included in page banner.

Historical Evolution & Declassified Case Studies

Chronological progression from early cold war military studies to modern legislative intelligence mandates.

1959 U.S. ARMY STUDY

Project Horizon (Lunar Outpost Study)

A secret 1959 military study conducted by the U.S. Army to assess the feasibility of establishing a military lunar base and space transportation system, designed to be operational by late 1965 with defensive capabilities.

> Declassified Directive Excerpt:

"No contacts with agencies outside the Army will be made until after the results of the preliminary investigation have been presented to the Department of Defense... no public release will be made concerning this project."

— Lt. Gen. Arthur G. Trudeau, Chief of Research & Development
1980s ACQUISITION ERA

"Black Programs" & Lockheed Skunk Works

Through the 1980s, close-hold programs protecting DoD acquisition were publicly referred to as "Black Programs." Key examples included Lockheed's Skunk Works development of stealth aircraft like the F-117A Nighthawk.

1980s MID-ERA OVERSIGHT CATALYST

Operation "Yellow Fruit" Investigation

A controversial covert special operations activity called "Yellow Fruit" became public, revealing alleged illegal use of funds and systemic lack of oversight. The subsequent court-martials and imprisonments prompted Congress to enforce strict oversight rules.

1990s SECURITY REFORM

Modern Special Access Program (SAP) Transition

The term "Special Access Program" officially replaced "Black Program." Security procedures expanded beyond hardware acquisition to encompass Intelligence Programs as well as Operations and Support Programs.

MODERN LEGISLATION STATUTORY MANDATES

Intelligence Authorization Act & Compartment Rules

Legislative frameworks (including H.R. 5412 and Intelligence Oversight IOO standards) prohibit heads of intelligence elements from establishing compartments or subcompartments under covered SAPs without prior notification to congressional intelligence committees.

Annual Requirement: Certification by DNI & SECDEF justifying creation, revalidation, or termination.
UAP Declassification Mandate: Federal agency records transfer directives (e.g. NARA AC 04.2025) for historical UAP records.

Essential FOIA Exemption Codes

(b)(1) Classified National Security Information

(b)(3) Exempt by Other Federal Statutes

Protects information specifically prohibited from disclosure by statutes (e.g. CIA Act of 1949, NSA Act).

(b)(6) Personal Privacy

Protects personnel, medical, and similar files whose disclosure would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of privacy.

(b)(7) Law Enforcement Records

Protects records compiled for law enforcement purposes, operational techniques, or confidential sources.
